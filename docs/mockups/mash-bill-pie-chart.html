Mash Bill Pie Chart

Today a Bourbon Library profile page's Mash Bill block is a single stacked bar (grainBarHtml in app.js) with a text legend underneath. This mockup proposes a donut chart with the percentage printed on every slice as the primary visualization, keeping a numeric legend beside it so the exact figures are never more than a glance away. Pick a bourbon on the left — the eight samples are chosen from the real 279-entry library to cover a 2-grain rye, a wheated bourbon, a 4-grain "triple malt" recipe, an all-non-corn mash bill, a smoked-malt specialty grain, and an unresearched placeholder.

Design notes

Why a donut instead of a plain pie

The hole leaves room for a center callout — the dominant grain's own percentage and name, the single fact a staff member glancing at the page is most likely to want first. A plain pie has no natural place to put that without covering a slice.

Where the number goes

A slice wide enough to hold its own label (roughly ≥12%) gets the percentage printed directly on it. Thinner slices — a 2% malted barley kicker, say — would be unreadable crammed into their own sliver, so every grain's exact percentage still lives in the legend beside the chart regardless of slice size, in the same bold tabular-numeral style either way.

Hover ties the two halves together

Hovering (or focusing) a slice highlights its legend row and vice versa — try it on the 4-grain Rabbit Hole Cavehill sample, where three slices sit close in size. Encodes the same "state in both form and number" idea the confidence badges already use elsewhere on this page.

The old bar isn't gone, just demoted

The existing stacked bar still appears — smaller, under a "Compare to bar" toggle in the block header — since it's what currently prints on the shelf talker itself (buildMashBillHtml in card.js) and staff may want the two side by side while this rolls out. The chart is a profile-page-only addition; nothing about what actually prints changes.

Open question: an extended grain color palette

MASH_BILL_GRAIN_COLORS today only covers six grains (Corn, Rye, Wheat, Malted Barley, Malted Rye, Oat) — enough for what a hand-typed shelf talker mash bill has ever used. The Bourbon Library's 279 researched entries introduced real grain names outside that list (Red Winter Wheat, Malted Wheat, Honey/Caramel Malted Barley, 6-Row Distiller's Malt, Mesquite Smoked Malt, plain "Barley", "Oats"), which currently all fall back to one flat grey swatch — fine on a 3-segment bar, but two different malted-barley variants sharing a color on the same pie chart reads as a bug, not a design choice. This mockup proposes the palette below: family-grouped tints so related grains stay visually related, plus one deliberately different color for genuinely different concepts (a smoked malt isn't "a shade of barley"). This needs sign-off before implementation, not something to decide silently in code.

The "Unknown" placeholder case

Heaven Hill Grain to Glass is a real library entry with no researched mash bill yet — its single "Corn 100%" row exists only because the schema requires at least one grain (see its confidence note in the seed data). A full donut ring in the Corn color would misrepresent that as a real 100%-corn recipe, so an unknown-tier entry instead renders the ring as a dashed, half-opacity placeholder with an explicit "not yet researched" note where the center callout would normally sit — select it on the left to see this.
